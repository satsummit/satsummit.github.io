The United States observes the Earth in extraordinary ways, through civilian satellites like Landsat, commercial imagery purchased by government agencies, and capabilities built for national security. Each was built for a different mission, governed by different rules, and designed for different users. Dan Opstal has spent his career working across those boundaries. At USGS, he helps lead the National Land Imaging Program, home to Landsat. Previously, through the Civil Applications Committee, he worked where the civilian, commercial, defense, and intelligence communities meet, bringing remote-sensing capabilities to disasters, wildfires, and environmental change. Dan takes us inside that world. What determines who can see, share, and act on imagery? Where have agencies found creative ways around institutional boundaries, and where do those boundaries still get in the way? As the number of satellites overhead explodes, he asks a bigger question: what would it take to treat Earth observation less like separate systems and more like shared infrastructure for understanding a changing planet?
